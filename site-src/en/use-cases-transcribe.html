Turn recordings and videos into text, on your Mac

Meeting recordings, lectures, interviews, and videos become timestamped text you can copy or export. The audio is recognized on your Mac and never uploaded.

{{updated}}

How to transcribe a file

  1. Click the Tidytalk icon in the menu bar and choose "Transcribe File or Link…".
  2. Drop an audio or video file into the window, or click "Choose File…". MP3, M4A, WAV, MP4, MOV and other common formats work, up to 3 hours long.
  3. Tidytalk splits the recording at pauses and recognizes it sentence by sentence. Each sentence shows when it was said.
  4. Click "Copy All", or "Export" to save it as plain text, SRT subtitles, or Markdown.

Transcribe a video link

Paste a link to a single video from YouTube, Bilibili, or another site, and click "Transcribe". The first time, Tidytalk downloads yt-dlp, an open-source download tool (about 35 MB), from its official GitHub release and checks it against the published checksum. After that, only the video's audio is downloaded, and it's deleted as soon as the transcript is done.

Use links only for content you have the right to use. Videos that need you to sign in, playlists, and live streams aren't supported.

Which speech model to use

File transcription uses the model you choose in Settings › Speech Model. All of them run on your Mac.

ModelBest for
SenseVoice (default)Fast transcripts in Chinese, English, Japanese, Korean, or Cantonese, including mixed Chinese and English.
Qwen3-ASRThe most accurate results and the best punctuation, in 52 languages. It also recognizes names and terms from your vocabulary.
Whisper TurboLess common languages; it covers about 99. Use another model for Chinese.

Export formats

  • Plain text (.txt): paragraphs split at longer pauses, ready for notes or an AI summary.
  • Subtitles (.srt): one cue per sentence with start and end times, for video editors and players.
  • Markdown (.md): the title and each sentence with its timestamp, for docs and wikis.

Tips and limits

  • Clear audio with one person speaking at a time gives the best results. Background music and people talking over each other lower accuracy.
  • Tidytalk doesn't label who is speaking.
  • Your replacements and Chinese–English spacing are applied to the transcript, just like dictation.
  • For a meeting that's happening right now, use live captions instead: press ⌃⌥C to caption what your Mac is playing as it plays.

Privacy

Recognition happens on your Mac, so recordings are never uploaded. Transcripts stay in the window until you copy or export them, and nothing is saved anywhere else. For links, the only network traffic is downloading yt-dlp once and the video's audio.
