Changelog

New features, fixes, and downloads for every Tidytalk release.

0.4.0 Latest

2026-10-28 · Download

  • A redesigned interface: the main window has four pages on the left, Home, Transcribe, Library, and Vocabulary (⌘1–⌘4), with Settings in the bottom-left corner. Every option lives in exactly one place, and empty pages tell you how to get started.
  • Home shows how much you dictated today and roughly how many minutes of typing that saved, cards for Dictation, Live Captions, and Meetings, and your recent results. If a permission or the speech model is missing, a banner at the top says what's left to do.
  • A new Transcribe page: drop in an audio or video file, or paste a video link, and your transcriptions are listed below. The title and cover show up as soon as Tidytalk reads them, before the audio downloads. A failed transcription can be retried in one click.
  • The Library puts dictation, meeting notes, and transcriptions in one list, grouped by day, with filters, search (⌘F), and sorting. Hover over an item to copy it, tidy it again, or delete it.
  • Dictation history is now saved on your Mac for 30 days by default, so you can search it in the Library. It's never uploaded, and Tidytalk doesn't record which app you pasted into. In Settings › Privacy & Data you can change how long it's kept, clear it, or turn saving off so it's cleared when you quit. If you're upgrading, the Library tells you about this once.
  • Vocabulary has four tabs, Terms, Replacements, Snippets, and Custom Rules, and changes save right away. Words you correct after pasting are suggested for your vocabulary.
  • Settings are grouped into Features, Shared, and System. Settings › General adds Appearance (System, Light, Dark) and Language (System, 简体中文, English).
  • Meeting details can play back the whole meeting, with your side and the other side together, and you can scrub and change the speed. Settings › Meetings adds a switch for telling speakers apart. During a meeting, both audio sources show a level meter, and Tidytalk tells you if it keeps hearing nothing from your Mac.
  • Detail pages have a back button in the top-left corner (⌘[), and long titles wrap. Meetings and transcriptions can be exported with File › Export… (⇧⌘E).
  • Property labels on meetings and transcriptions are no longer cut off in English.

Install: requires macOS 26 or later on a Mac with Apple silicon. The app isn't notarized. The first time you open it, click "Open Anyway" in System Settings › Privacy & Security, or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Tidytalk.app.

0.3.2

2026-10-28 · Download

  • Settings now live in the main window instead of a separate window. Click Settings in the bottom-left corner of the main window or press ⌘, and the sidebar switches to the settings pages. Click "‹ Settings" or press ⌘[ to go back to where you were. Settings reopen on the page you last viewed. The Vocabulary page in Settings duplicated the one in the main window and has been removed.

  • Privacy policy: the website has a new privacy policy that explains what Tidytalk stores on your Mac, when it goes online, and which text is sent to the AI service you set up. The privacy section in Settings › General links to it.
  • Settings › General: System Audio Recording now says it's used for meeting notes as well as live captions.

0.3.1

2026-10-26 · Download

  • Bluetooth headsets recording nothing (#1): with AirPods or another Bluetooth headset as the microphone, the headset first switches to call mode, and recording often stopped right then and captured nothing. The microphone is now reopened automatically when it stops or goes quiet for too long, and recording carries on.
  • Tidytalk tells you when nothing was recorded: if you held the key and spoke but no audio came in, the indicator no longer just disappears. It explains why, and with a Bluetooth headset it suggests using the Mac's microphone.
  • You can see when the microphone is ready: until audio arrives, the level bars in the indicator dim and pulse slowly. With a Bluetooth headset, the start sound plays once the microphone is actually listening, so you won't lose your first words.
  • Settings › Dictation: when the microphone in use is a Bluetooth headset, Tidytalk explains what that means, and a button next to it switches to the Mac's built-in microphone.
  • Update prompt: very long release notes made the prompt taller than the screen, so its buttons were out of reach. The notes now scroll inside the prompt, in your interface language only.

Install: requires macOS 26 or later on a Mac with Apple silicon. The app isn't notarized. The first time you open it, click "Open Anyway" in System Settings › Privacy & Security, or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Tidytalk.app.

0.3.0

2026-10-26 · Download

  • Meeting notes: press ⌃⌥M to record your microphone and the meeting audio on your Mac at the same time. A rough transcript appears during the meeting, and afterwards speakers are separated on your Mac, even when several people are on the other side, with overlapping speech marked. Rename speakers or re-split them by a set number of people. Generate an AI summary (overview, key points, decisions, and action items); Tidytalk tells you where the text will be sent first. Recordings and transcripts stay on this Mac.
  • A new main window: meetings, transcriptions, recent results, and vocabulary in one place. The interface is cleaner and more compact, buttons are icons with descriptions and shortcuts on hover, and Settings and the setup guide use the same style.
  • Transcription history: every file or link you transcribe is saved as a card you can search, play back, jump to by timestamp, edit by double-clicking a line, and export. Audio and cover images downloaded from links stay on your Mac.
  • API keys in the Keychain: keys are no longer written to the config file. A key already in the file moves to the Keychain the next time you save a setting.
  • Steadier dictation: recording continues when your microphone changes mid-recording (AirPods switching, a USB mic unplugged); press Esc to cancel while waiting for AI; you can start the next dictation right after pasting; and Tidytalk no longer freezes when the target app does.
  • Your config file is safe: if it has a syntax error, the menu says so at the top and opens the file with one click, and edits made in your editor are no longer overwritten by the Settings window.
  • Speech models: a damaged model shows why and can be downloaded again; disk space is checked before downloading, a failed checksum moves on to the next mirror, and switching models frees the old one first instead of keeping both in memory.
  • AI tidying: the timeout grows with longer dictation; short questions are no longer answered by the AI; translations can't come back unchanged, and French, Spanish, and other accented translations now pass; Japanese no longer gets Chinese spacing rules; and local vocabulary fixes no longer change common English and Chinese words (such as discard → Discord).
  • Live captions: you're prompted to allow "System Audio Recording" when it's missing; listening continues after plugging in headphones or AirPods switching modes; and ⌃⌥V is only taken while captions are on.
  • File transcription: you can stop during decoding, long files use far less memory, and yt-dlp updates itself after 14 days so link transcription keeps working when sites change.
  • The app is now signed with a fixed certificate, so other programs can't borrow its permissions and upgrades keep your permissions. The installer only includes what Apple silicon needs, so it's smaller.

Install: requires macOS 26 or later on a Mac with Apple silicon. The app isn't notarized. The first time you open it, click "Open Anyway" in System Settings › Privacy & Security, or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Tidytalk.app.

0.2.2

2026-10-23 · Download

  • More reliable pasting: your clipboard is restored only after the target app has actually read the text, instead of after a fixed delay, so a busy app no longer pastes what you copied before.
  • Faster results for long dictation: what you've already said is recognized while you keep talking, so only the last part is left when you let go. With Qwen3-ASR, a 40-second recording is ready about 1 second after release instead of about 5.7, with the same result.
  • Vocabulary fixes on your Mac: different capitalization or spacing (github → GitHub), a letter or two off in long English words, and Chinese homophones of terms three characters or longer, even with tidying off. Words in links, email addresses, and paths aren't touched.
  • Reasoning models just work: with Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1, and similar models in Ollama, Tidytalk asks the service to skip thinking and removes any thinking from the reply. A cut-off reply pastes the original text instead of half a sentence.
  • New tidytalk:// links for Raycast, Shortcuts, and Karabiner: tidytalk://toggle starts or finishes dictation, tidytalk://cancel cancels, and tidytalk://captions turns live captions on or off.

Install: requires macOS 26 or later on a Mac with Apple silicon. The app isn't notarized. The first time you open it, click "Open Anyway" in System Settings › Privacy & Security, or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Tidytalk.app.
