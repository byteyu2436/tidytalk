Tidytalk vs Wispr Flow vs Superwhisper vs macOS Dictation

Four ways to talk instead of type on a Mac. Here's how they differ on price, privacy, and what happens to your words.

Updated 2026-10-19

Tidytalk is our project, so we've kept this page factual. Competitor details come from their official websites and help pages, checked in October 2026. Prices and features change, so check each product's site before you decide.

Tidytalk

Tidytalk is paragraph-style: you hold a shortcut, say a whole thought, and let go. Speech is recognized on your Mac with the SenseVoice model, then an AI service you pick removes fillers and slips and adds punctuation before the text is pasted at your cursor.

  • You control where text goes. Use DeepSeek, OpenAI, or any OpenAI-compatible service with your own API key, or a local model in Ollama or LM Studio so nothing leaves your Mac. Your voice is never uploaded.
  • Free and open source. No word limits or subscriptions. With a cloud AI service, you pay the provider directly for what you use.
  • Strong for mixed Chinese and English. It handles English terms inside Chinese speech, adds Chinese–English spacing, and can translate as it tidies.
  • Limits to know about: Mac only, and only macOS 26 or later on Apple silicon. It recognizes five spoken languages: Chinese, English, Japanese, Korean, and Cantonese. The app isn't notarized yet, so you approve it once in System Settings. Using AI cleanup means setting up an API key or a local model.

Wispr Flow

Wispr Flow is a polished commercial app that turns speech into clean writing in any app, and it learns your names and jargon. It runs on Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android, which makes it the easiest choice if you dictate across devices.

  • The free plan allows 2,000 words a week on desktop and 1,000 on mobile. Pro removes the limit for $15 a month, or $12 a month billed yearly.
  • According to Wispr's own help center, transcription always runs in the cloud. Privacy settings control model training and storage, not where audio is processed, so there's no offline mode.

Looking for something like Wispr Flow that's free and runs on your Mac? See Tidytalk as a Wispr Flow alternative.

Superwhisper

Superwhisper is a feature-rich commercial app with configurable AI modes that reformat what you say, from casual messages to structured notes. It runs on Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android.

  • It can transcribe offline with local models, which its FAQ says run best on Apple silicon Macs. Cloud models are also available.
  • The free plan includes unlimited dictation with local Whisper models. AI modes, cloud models, and file transcription need Pro: $8.49 a month, $84.99 a year, or $249.99 once. Pro's local AI model works in English only.

See Tidytalk as a Superwhisper alternative for a closer comparison.

macOS Dictation

Dictation is built into macOS, free, and needs no setup. On Apple silicon Macs, supported languages are processed on the device. It types your words as you speak, so it's great for quick input, but it keeps fillers and doesn't clean up or restructure what you said. It stops automatically after 30 seconds of silence.

Which one should you choose?

  • Choose Tidytalk if you want a free, open-source tool, care where your text is sent, or dictate in Chinese mixed with English.
  • Choose Wispr Flow if you want the same dictation on Windows and your phone and don't mind cloud processing or a subscription.
  • Choose Superwhisper if you want lots of customizable AI modes and offline models on several platforms, and are happy to pay for Pro.
  • Stick with macOS Dictation if you only need quick, word-for-word input and don't need fillers removed.
