How to make dictation get names and technical terms right

Speech recognition is good at everyday words and bad at the words that matter most to you: your colleagues' names, your product, your tools. Tidytalk gives you four ways to fix that, from the moment you speak to the text that's pasted.

Updated 2026-10-28

Everything here is on the Vocabulary page of the main window.

1. List your common words

Under Common Words, write the names, products, and terms you use, one per line. For example:

Kubernetes
Figma
Claude
SenseVoice

These words help at two stages:

  • While recognizing: with Qwen3-ASR as the speech model (Settings › Speech Model), these words are recognized correctly from the start. This works even with tidying off.
  • While tidying: with any model, the list is given to the AI, so a similar-sounding mistake is corrected to your spelling. This needs "Tidy up text" on.

2. Add replacements for repeat mistakes

If a word is always misheard the same way, add a replacement, for example "cloud" → "Claude". English matching ignores case and only matches whole words, so "cloudy" is left alone. Replacements run on your Mac and work even with tidying off.

The quickest way to add one: open Recent Results in the menu bar menu, pick the result that went wrong, and choose "Add to Vocabulary…".

3. Let it learn from your corrections

When you fix a word yourself right after pasting, in the same text field, Tidytalk notices. The change is listed under "Words you changed" on the Vocabulary page. Nothing is added until you click ✓.

It only compares within about a minute and a half of pasting and saves nothing. You can turn it off at the bottom of the Vocabulary page.

4. Write rules for how things are written

Custom Rules control style rather than spelling, one per line, up to 20. For example:

  • Write amounts as digits.
  • Write the product name as Tidytalk.
  • Write times in 24-hour format.

Tidying and voice rewrite follow them. Rules only change how things are written; they never make the AI add content.

Bonus: snippets

Say only a short phrase such as "my email" to paste a full email address, an address, or a canned reply. A snippet is used only when the whole recording is that phrase, never when it comes up mid-sentence.

Which one should I use?

ProblemFix
A name or term is misspelled in different waysCommon Words, ideally with Qwen3-ASR
The same wrong word appears every timeReplacements
You keep fixing words after pastingLet Edits Found suggest them
Spelling is right, but the style isn'tCustom Rules
You type the same long text again and againSnippets
